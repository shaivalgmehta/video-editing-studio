### LinkedIn
Most brands want to rank in AI search for everything they sell. Leslie Ann Hall, Founder & CEO of Iced Media, says that's the first mistake.

Her starting point: "AI search is a mirror for what the world thinks about your brand." If your teams tell different stories, AI notices.

Her three rules:
→ Pick a handful of hero products. Brands often arrive with 200, but "they might not deserve to rank for everything."
→ One message for every team: organic, paid, PR, the people merchandising your site and the people running Amazon PDPs.
→ Content "understood by both humans and machines": a crawlable, well-structured site, backed by the right creators and PR.

Where AI gets its answers in beauty, per Iced Media: Reddit, YouTube, PR mentions on rankings and "best of" lists, and TikTok and TikTok Shop. Her caveat: "It's changing every day," so don't depend on one source.

### LinkedIn
A lot of "how search works" thinking is out of date. Leslie Ann Hall of Iced Media took five common beliefs apart on The Retail Razor Show:

1. Belief: AI search is a new channel. She treats it as the foundation under every channel.
2. Belief: legacy brands own search. SEO rewarded decades of history; "GEO is looking at fresh, new, and dynamic content," which gives indie and challenger brands a real opening.
3. Belief: rank for every product. Focus on "a handful of hero products" you have the right to win.
4. Belief: put more budget into conversion. Iced Media, a performance agency, calls it "bottom funnel bloat": paying more to get existing customers to buy more often, at the expense of finding new ones.
5. Belief: creator post → add to cart. Shoppers now add a validation step in between, on Reddit and the LLMs.

### LinkedIn
Five questions Ricardo Belmar asked Leslie Ann Hall, Founder & CEO of Iced Media, at Shoptalk Fall, and her answers:

What should brands do as AI changes discovery?
Make AI search the foundation, not one more channel, and tell one consistent story everywhere.

Has brand marketing been overlooked?
Yes. "Those vibes are more important than ever," while too much spend has drifted to the bottom of the funnel.

Is the trust factor changing?
Yes. There's a new validation stage where shoppers ask, "But is this the best product for me?"

How important has Reddit become?
"Reddit is wildly important." By Leslie's figure, "over half of Redditors do what they call a final check on Reddit before they make a purchase."

Should brands treat every channel equally?
No. Consider the whole ecosystem and go where your customers spend their time. Ricardo: "That's an important distinction."
